Rhai Sales Motion: 
Not sales but a MOTION for sales: it should be a constant engine that’s running and in predictable ways, adapted to the terrainMARKETING FUNNEL: how do we do high reach, low touch activities to meet a lot of people easily and then what do we do to move them down step by step to higher touch steps? Finally to convert them to a sale and then retain them to sell to them again and again? 

TOP OF FUNNEL: 
Discovery surface: 
Content 
Free community events 
Making things other people will share 
Makes them a champion somehow 

Before we get on a call to sell/demo we need to qualify the lead and the people in the call. By the time we get to a meeting, it should be to go ahead with a sale (proposal ready, shared, demo somewhat ready). Not just for discovery - that’s too high touch. 
MIDDLE: 
Sending an NDA proactively - not just before the call but how soon after they give us the legal name do we send that document? The question they should have: how the hell are they doing this so quickly? 
Discovery process 
Qualifying before the call
Research
Relevant examples to show them 
Asking the right, industry level questions 
Selling to that person and their interest level
Getting to a budget number without it feeling sales-y
Hand holding here 
Using AI to extract information
Making them feel that this data room will be easy to put together, aiding them in getting the information out, workshop/meetings with their team
Not demanding info but helping them get it out, making the company legible is our job 
WhatsApp group management: what can we do here to make it a positive touch point? 
What needs to be on email - proposal, approval, invoicing, pre email and post email 
Coordinating bookings for hotel, stay 
Connecting them to others 
Goodwill, learning more of the context they work in 
Converting them quickly to a session
Intent and budget 
Face time with the team
The sales pitch becomes paid  
Get some buy in so they’re invested 
Documents 
Time in discovery
Their team members buy in 
Invoicing cleanly and well is also a step in this
Every touch point should work on both speed and the personalised touch and thoroughness 
Closing email: upselling, giving them a clear takeaway, next steps etc 

BOTTOM OF THE FUNNEL:
Session
Tech build
Reels, blogs on the engagement
Voice feedback for website 
MAKE PEOPLE YOUR CHAMPION
Upsell to other products 
Share with their network 

Leads need to be qualified on three criteria: 
Intent: do they have the intent to do something with AI? 
Inbound is the clearest way for us to know that they are interested 
Some of our outbounds: they were a slow no, which is the worst because it takes a lot of our time and energy. 
Inbound isn’t the only kind of intent signal of course but that’s where with outbound qualifying a lead is very important. Do they have a budget for this? Who will be a champion? How easy is it to schedule a call for this? All act as signals. 
Passive intent vs. active intent: are they actually going to put aside the money? Will they be responsive and push this through? 
Ability: 
Do they internally have the ability to approve budgets? Up to what amount?
Do they have the ability to get people in the company together? Up to what level, how can we help them with this (upwards or downwards) 
Urvi for example doesn’t yet have this in her org, Silpa did but to a degree and so it got pushed a bit. Proportion of person to org too. 
Do they understand the value proposition – given we do our job of explaining it to the best of our ability? 
SRC for example – they’re not at a level we are at the ability to communicate to 
Stage: 
Size of the company and the complexity of the problem
Some are too large - it’s actually too hard to push this through. Dodla was this, kind of. 
Which stage of their AI journey are they on?
Century for example is pretty far along, so its a question of a lot of competition
Halol was perfect - primed with all the data, knew the market price so we could pick them off with the right price and the right solution
Overall tech journey 
SRC for example was too far behind on this 
Matching
There are some industries with too many out of the box solutions 
Too many data restrictions or security requirements - banking, health, education to a degree, security like airports, governments etc.
