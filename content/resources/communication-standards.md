# How We Write at Rhai

**A guide for everyone joining the team.**

---

## Why this document exists

Rhai sells one thing before it sells anything else: **the impression that we are competent, careful, and worth trusting with a company's data.**

Our clients are listed companies, promoters, CTOs and family-business owners — people who are deciding whether to hand us their ERP, their customer database, and ₹5–25 lakhs. Almost every one of those decisions gets made partly on the basis of how we write. A sloppy message doesn't just read as casual; it reads as *this is how they'll handle my project.*

You will be writing to clients, in client WhatsApp groups, on social media, and in outbound messages to people who have never heard of us. There is no "internal only" writing here. The internal WhatsApp group is practice for the client group, and the habits you build in one carry into the other automatically.

**The standard is simple:** every message you send should be one you'd be comfortable with a client reading.

## Part 1 — The non-negotiables

These are not stylistic preferences. Getting these wrong is the fastest way to look junior.

### 1. Capital letters

Start every sentence with a capital letter. Capitalise "I". Capitalise names.

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| hey rhea, i've added the docs | Hi Rhea, I've added the docs |
| do i have to fill this form as well? | Do I need to fill this form as well? |
| have added all the required docs here | I've added all the required documents here |

Lowercase-everything reads as texting a friend. It is the single most common tell of an intern who hasn't made the shift to professional writing, and it is the easiest thing in this document to fix.

### 2. Write words out

No SMS abbreviations. Ever — not internally, not to us, not anywhere.

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| lmk | Let me know / I'll let you know |
| lemme know | Let me know |
| btw | Also / By the way |
| okzz, kk, k | Got it / Understood / Yes, that works |
| pls | Please |
| ur, u | Your, you |
| asap | By [specific time] |

"okzz" is the clearest example of the problem. It takes the same time to type as "Got it", but one of them says *I am taking this seriously* and the other says *I am on my phone and half-paying-attention.*

### 3. Punctuation in direct address

When you address someone by name, it takes a comma.

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| Okay thanks for sharing Rhea. | Thanks for sharing, Rhea. |
| Sounds great Rhea. | Sounds great, Rhea. |
| Hi Abhilash have added the docs | Hi Abhilash, I've added the documents. |

### 4. "Looking forward to" takes a gerund

You look forward **to meeting** someone, not "to meet" them. This one shows up constantly and it's worth memorising.

| Don't | Do |
|---|---|
| Looking forward to meet you guys | Looking forward to meeting you |
| Looking forward to meet you people on Monday | Looking forward to meeting everyone on Monday |

Also: "you people" reads as cold or even confrontational in Indian English, though it's rarely intended that way. Use "everyone", "the team", or just "you".

### 5. One message, not five

Compose your full thought, then send it. Four consecutive messages over five minutes buzzes someone's phone four times and makes a simple reply feel like an event.

**Don't:**
> Sounds great Rhea.
> Thank you for sharing the location. I will bring my own laptop.
> Sure, sounds good, looking forward to meet you people on Monday! :)
> btw by what time should i arrive on Monday lemme know?

**Do:**
> Thanks, Rhea. I'll bring my own laptop and I've joined both groups. What time should I arrive on Monday?

Same content, one buzz, and it reads as someone who thought before typing.

## Part 2 — Acknowledging vs confirming

This is the most important section in this document, and it's the difference between an intern who needs managing and one who can be trusted with clients.

**Acknowledging** means saying you saw the message. **Confirming** means saying what you did about it.

When someone gives you a task or a link, "Sure" and "Okay thanks" tell them nothing. They now have to remember to check whether you did it. That means you have *added* work to their day rather than removing it.

| Situation | Acknowledgement (weak) | Confirmation (right) |
|---|---|---|
| Sent two WhatsApp group links | "Okay thanks for sharing" | "Joined both groups, thanks." |
| Asked for documents | "Sure." | "Sent — Aadhaar, PAN, passbook and certificates are in the Dropbox folder. Let me know if anything's missing." |
| Given a meeting time | "Sure, works!" | "7:30 works, I'll be ready." |
| Asked to complete a form | "Sure." | "Form's filled and uploaded to the same folder." |

The test: **after reading your reply, does the other person still need to check on you?** If yes, you've acknowledged but not confirmed.

### Close the loop without being asked

If you said you'd do something, report back when it's done. Not a week later — the same day, ideally within the hour. Nobody should ever have to ask "did you do X?"

## Part 3 — Asking questions well

Questions are welcome. Vague ones aren't, and neither are questions you could have answered yourself in thirty seconds.

**Do the obvious thing rather than asking whether to do it.** If you've been sent an onboarding form, fill it in. Asking "do I have to fill this form as well?" and then filling it in half an hour later means you sent a message that cost someone attention and changed nothing.

**Bundle your questions.** If you have three, send them in one message as a short list, rather than as they occur to you across an afternoon.

**Make questions specific and answerable.**

| Vague | Specific |
|---|---|
| "I'd love to know more about these and all the info I need to get started" | "For outbound — should I be working from an existing list, or building one? And is there a template you'd like me to start from?" |
| "lmk if anything else needed" | "That's everything on the checklist. Anything else you need from me before Monday?" |

**When you're stuck, say what you tried.** "I couldn't access the dashboard — I tried logging in with the email you sent and got an error at the password step" is useful. "It's not working" is not.

## Part 4 — Response times

| Situation | Reply within |
|---|---|
| Client message (any channel) | 2 hours during working hours, always same day |
| Rhea, Yeshoda or Shriya asking something direct | Same day |
| A long message laying out options or a decision | 24 hours |
| Something you can't answer yet | Same day, with a holding reply |

The holding reply matters. If someone sends you a detailed message and you need time to think, say so:

> "Thanks — reading through this properly and I'll come back to you tomorrow morning with a proper answer."

Two days of silence after a message that took someone twenty minutes to write reads as disinterest, even when you're actually thinking carefully about it. One sentence prevents that.

## Part 5 — Tone

We are warm, direct, and unfussy. Not stiff, not corporate, not casual.

### What we sound like

- **Warm without being gushy.** "Thanks, this is really helpful" — not "Thank you so so much!!! 🙏🙏"
- **Direct without being blunt.** Say the thing. Don't bury it in four sentences of throat-clearing.
- **Confident without overclaiming.** If you don't know, say you don't know and say when you'll find out.
- **Plain.** Short sentences. Ordinary words. We are explaining AI to people who don't work in tech — jargon is a failure, not a flex.

### What we don't sound like

- **Over-apologetic.** "So sorry to bother you again!!" — you're not bothering anyone, you're doing your job. Once is a courtesy; every message is a tic.
- **Over-familiar.** "hey hey" is for friends. "Hi Rhea" or "Hi everyone" is for work.
- **Padded.** "I just wanted to quickly check if maybe you had a chance to possibly look at…" → "Have you had a chance to look at this?"
- **Emoji-heavy.** One at the end of a genuinely warm message is fine. Never in a client message unless the client used one first. Never more than one.

### Exclamation marks

One per message, maximum, and only when there's real warmth or good news. Two or more reads as anxious. Zero is often correct.

## Part 6 — Channel by channel

### Internal WhatsApp (this group)

Slightly relaxed, but every rule in Part 1 still applies. Capitals, full words, complete sentences. Confirm rather than acknowledge. This group is practice.

### Client WhatsApp groups

You are Rhai here, not yourself. Full sentences, no abbreviations, no emoji unless mirrored. Never send a message here that hasn't been read back once before sending.

If you're unsure whether something should go to a client, ask first. It is always cheaper to check than to unsend.

### Email

Subject lines that say what the email is about — "Renaissance — revised dashboard spec attached", not "Update" or "Hi".

Structure: greeting, one line of context, the substance, a clear ask or next step, sign-off. If there's an action for the recipient, it goes on its own line so it can't be missed.

Sign off as: your name, then "Rhai" on the next line.

### Outbound / cold messages

Short. Specific to them. No template smell. If you couldn't say why *this* person is getting *this* message, don't send it.

Every cold message gets read by Rhea before it goes out, until we agree otherwise.

### Social media

Different rules — this is the one place where the brand voice is looser. Still no typos, still no "lemme". Drafts get reviewed before posting until you've got the voice.

## Part 7 — The read-back rule

**Before you send anything, read it once, out loud if you can.**

You are checking for:

1. Does every sentence start with a capital?
2. Are there any abbreviations to expand?
3. Have I confirmed, or only acknowledged?
4. Is this one message or should it have been one message?
5. Is there a clear next step, and is it obvious whose it is?
6. Would I be comfortable if a client read this?

This takes about eight seconds and it will fix ninety per cent of what's in this document.

## Part 8 — Worked examples

**Being added to a new group**

> Don't: hey hey rhea, yeshoda, shriya, looking forward to meet you guys. thank you for welcoming! and sure lemme know about the onboarding docs.

> Do: Hi Rhea, Yeshoda and Shriya — thanks for the welcome, looking forward to working with you. I'll watch out for the onboarding documents; happy to send anything you need from my side in the meantime.

**Receiving links and instructions**

> Don't: Okay thanks for sharing Rhea.

> Do: Thanks, Rhea — I've joined both groups. I'll go through the orientation docs when they land on Monday.

**Confirming logistics**

> Don't: okzz

> Do: Got it, see you at 10:30.

**Sending documents**

> Don't: hi have added all the required docs here. adhaar, pan, bank passbook, 10th mark sheet, btech certificate, offer letter of prev employment. lmk if anything else needed.

> Do: Hi Abhilash, I've uploaded everything to this folder: [link]
>
> Aadhaar, PAN, bank passbook, 10th marksheet, B.Tech certificate, and my previous offer letter. I've also filled in the onboarding form and added it to the same folder. Let me know if anything else is needed.

**Following up on something you haven't heard back on**

> Don't: hey just checking on this?

> Do: Hi Rhea — following up on the outbound list from Tuesday. No rush if you're travelling, just flagging it so it doesn't get lost.
