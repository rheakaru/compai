Hester Biosciences makes animal vaccines and health products out of Mehsana, Gujarat — forty years old, listed, roughly ₹2,000 crore. I went in expecting the usual adoption story: a sceptical old-economy company that needs convincing about AI. That's not what I found.

The leadership was already sold. What they had instead was a start-stop problem — tools arriving with real momentum at the top, then stalling on the floor because nobody in the middle ever became responsible for making them work. The constraint wasn't motivation. It was ownership distribution. So the job was to build more owners.

This is the field-note version of that day: why a great demo makes spectators rather than owners, how a page built for each person out of their own words kills the blank-page problem, and why a workshop that isn't captured is just a nice day out.
